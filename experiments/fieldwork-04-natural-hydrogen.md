## Research question

**Could natural geologic hydrogen become a significant, economically recoverable energy resource, and what do we actually know about how it is generated, migrates, accumulates, escapes, and can be detected?**

Treat this as a live scientific and engineering controversy, not an invitation to write a promotional overview. Investigate the frontier as deeply as your research capabilities allow. You are responsible for finding and choosing sources, following promising leads, and identifying competing interpretations. Do not assume the answer is yes.

## What a useful report should accomplish

- Explain the physical and geochemical mechanisms proposed to produce hydrogen in Earth's crust and mantle, and which mechanisms have meaningful evidence behind them.
- Distinguish *hydrogen generation* from *a recoverable reservoir*. Examine transport, microbial consumption, trapping, leakage, replenishment, and measurement uncertainty.
- Investigate real-world occurrences, exploration campaigns, field measurements, drilling results, and production claims. Separate demonstrated observations from projections and marketing.
- Evaluate the strongest arguments **for and against** a commercially important resource, including scale, rates, extraction economics, safety, environmental effects, and comparison with manufactured hydrogen.
- Identify unresolved scientific questions, disputed estimates, weak evidence, and important negative results. Trace striking claims to their original sources when possible.
- Propose a small number of high-value, falsifiable measurements or experiments that would most reduce uncertainty. Explain what different outcomes would mean.

## Research discipline

Use independent external research. Prioritize primary papers, geological surveys, technical reports, measured field data, and credible critical analyses. Follow citations beyond summaries where possible. Distinguish peer-reviewed findings, preprints, institutional estimates, company claims, and your own inference. Cross-check pivotal numerical claims; include units, dates, and assumptions. Do not invent citations or present inaccessible sources as read.

Do not confine yourself to a predetermined list of websites or papers. Search broadly and pursue surprising but relevant evidence. If a source cannot be accessed or a claim cannot be verified, disclose the limitation rather than filling the gap with confident prose.

## Deliverable

Write a **substantial, self-contained technical intelligence report**, not a short answer. Use clear headings, with enough depth to teach a technically sophisticated reader something new. Organize the report around:

1. Executive assessment: what is known, what is plausible, what remains unsupported.
2. Mechanisms and subsurface system: how a working natural-hydrogen system would actually function.
3. Evidence map: important observations, case studies, quantitative estimates, and contradictions.
4. Competing hypotheses and failure modes: where the field may be fooling itself.
5. Economics and practical constraints: what would need to be true for deployment at scale.
6. Decisive unknowns: research priorities and falsifiable tests.
7. Source ledger: direct links to the most important external sources, with a short note on what each actually supports.

You may improve this structure if the evidence warrants it. Prefer explanatory synthesis over an indiscriminate catalog. State an explicit confidence level for major conclusions and make clear which claims are facts, estimates, or conjectures.

Do not artificially limit the length to a few paragraphs. Do not claim exhaustive coverage if research access or time was limited. The goal is a rigorous, surprising, useful report, not obedience theater.
